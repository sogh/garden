`nature-sim` started as a simulation of a Pacific Northwest meadow — grass,
flowers, butterflies, a real solar arc, weather that comes in off the water.
Something to watch rather than something to play. It is now closer to a
point-and-click adventure with a walker in it, and the meadow is one of five
places she can get between.

## The pillars it started with

Four of these are still load-bearing, and they're why the thing went where it
went rather than sprawling.

**No external art assets of any kind.** Not one sprite sheet, not one texture —
every pixel of 2D art and all the 3D geometry is built in Rust. Everything
below was drawn by code.

**Everything tunable from `config/meadow.yaml`**, read at startup, no
recompile.

**Bevy ECS** for simulation state — components, resources, systems.

**The simulation does not know how it is drawn.** Clock, solar position,
weather, phenology and habitat live at the crate root and stay
renderer-agnostic; anything touching a camera or a framebuffer lives in `two/`
or `three/`. `CLAUDE.md` calls this "the split to protect," and I think that's
the pillar that let the adventure happen — the meadow sim never had to know it
had become a backdrop.

**The pixel view is the focus, the 3D one may lag.** Permission to let the
first-person renderer fall behind rather than hold the pixel places back.

## What it turned into

The reference now is explicitly a Monkey Island map: `docs/map.md` wants "a
picture of the areas with the paths between them," found on foot before you can
travel by it.

The five binaries — `meadow2d`, `cave`, `town`, `bridge`, `bakery` — are the
same program. They differ only in which place is on screen when it opens, and
you can walk between all of them.

And then there's the bridge, which is not a sailing ship.

`CLAUDE.md` puts it plainly: *the meadow is planet one*. There's a `Surface`
interface a renderer reads so it can draw any world, and `docs/worlds.md` has
an addressing scheme above it that goes up to galaxies. So the meadow didn't
get replaced — it got a coordinate.

Two things I like that aren't visible in a screenshot: stop giving her input
and after a few seconds she carries on by herself, and the survey harness is
deterministic, so the same seed gives a byte-identical report and "is that five
points noise or signal" stops being a question.

It's been quiet since the 14th — sixty-one commits between the 8th and the
14th, one day of twenty-nine, then nothing. Stopped mid-feature, not at a
finish line.
