`voxel-artist` is a small Rust crate from a week in September 2025 (14 commits,
about 640 lines in `src/`). The whole pipeline is: fill a `VoxelGrid` with
colours from procedural shapes, shoot one ray per pixel through it on the CPU,
write a PNG (or a GIF, as of the fifth day). No meshes, no GPU, no assets.

The part I like is how little a shape has to be. `Shape` is one method,
`color_at(x, y, z) -> Option<Color>`, and `rasterize_shape` just asks it about
every voxel centre. A sphere is a distance check, and the cone and pyramid
(added later) are "how wide am I at this height." Later shapes overwrite
earlier ones, so a scene is just the order you call things in.

Rendering is a 3D DDA: clip the ray to the grid's bounding box, then step
voxel to voxel, always crossing whichever axis boundary comes next, until
something is filled. The nice side effect is that the axis you last stepped
across *is* the surface normal, so shading comes for free. It's ambient plus
one Lambert term plus optional exponential fog on hit distance. Every face
points along one of six directions, so it can only take one of six
brightnesses, and that's why the spheres get those terraced contour rings
instead of smooth shading. I think that's most of what gives it the look.

Because `Shape` is a public trait, you can write your own without touching
the library. These next three are scenes written for this post (they live as
extra examples in a scratch clone, not in the repo). A heightfield `Shape`
with colour bands by height, plus `Cone` trees:

That's the same trade the [[greedy-mesher]] makes: it starts from the same idea
(a grid filled from maths at voxel centres) and turns it into polygons for a
game engine, where this one never leaves the grid and just raycasts it. Both
are the same itch as [[the-meadow-grew-a-map]], drawing things from code
rather than making assets. There's also a longer walkthrough sitting in the
repo as `BLOG.md`.

A couple of loose ends I noticed. The comment on `RenderSettings::light_dir`
says it points *toward* the light, but the shader uses `n · -light_dir`, so it
actually behaves as the direction the light travels. The `sphere` example
follows the comment and negates it, so I think its light sweep is lighting the
faces pointing *away* from where the light is meant to be (from below, too). And the README's
usage snippet builds `RenderSettings` without the light and fog fields added
later, so it wouldn't compile as written. No shadows either, which `BLOG.md`
already lists as a next step.
